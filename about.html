My ties to Chile

I grew up in Chile and was educated there. I also served a mission in the south of Chile, where I fell in love with the landscapes of Patagonia.

Chilean literature

I have read Gabriela Mistral since I was a child. She was a Chilean poet and teacher born in 1889, and in 1945 she became the first Latin American to win the Nobel Prize in Literature. Roberto Bolaño (1953 to 2003) is also one of my favorite authors. I especially love his novels Distant Star (Estrella distante) and The Savage Detectives (Los detectives salvajes).

One interesting thing about Chile's physical geography

Chile's soils change dramatically from north to south. In the northern desert they are salty mixes of sand and gravel that are hard to farm even with irrigation. In the central valleys, river and volcanic deposits create rich, fertile soils with excellent farming potential. In the far south, constant cold and rain leave thin soils that support rainforest, moorland and little else (Encyclopaedia Britannica, n.d.).

How the Earth testifies of a Creator

When I visited the south of Chile, the mountains and lakes left me in awe. The beauty and vastness of that landscape reminded me of the Creator's love and power. I also see God's hand in the natural processes that continually shape the Earth, from the movement of tectonic plates to the flow of rivers. For me, the Earth's complexity, order, and beauty are powerful witnesses of a Supreme Creator.
